Biggest Constraint & Biggest Opportunity

You'll discuss these in the position breakouts — bring a proposed solution for your constraint.
Biggest constraint
I would recommend removing the Branch Manager sales goal as well as the logged praise goal.

From an operational standpoint, sales blocks require approximately 8 to 12 hours per week of a Branch Manager's time. That is time that could otherwise be invested in coaching, planning, hiring, operational execution, and addressing the day to day needs of the branch. Shifting that focus back to branch leadership would have a greater impact on overall performance.

Regarding praise, I believe recognition is most effective when it is authentic and occurs naturally. Establishing a quota for logged praise risks turning something that should be genuine into a compliance exercise, which can diminish its value.

Additionally, using logged praise as a leadership performance metric presents challenges. There is no practical way to verify whether every logged interaction occurred, making it difficult to evaluate consistently or fairly. If leadership performance is going to be measured in this area, it would be more effective to use metrics tied to observable outcomes, such as team engagement, retention, development, or performance, rather than the volume of logged entries.

There is also an administrative cost associated with documenting every instance of praise. In my view, that time is better spent engaging directly with the team. Programs like Legends, which recognize exceptional contributions, provide meaningful value and make sense to document. Routine individual praise, however, should remain an organic part of leadership rather than a tracked quota.
Biggest opportunity
I tend to take a lot of pride in things that sit outside my role because I enjoy finding ways to make the business better. I think my opportunity is to shift my mindset a bit, reel that back in, and focus more on what's actually being asked of me.

Where I felt I was succeeding by taking on extra initiatives and contributing in other areas, it's become clear to me that those efforts aren't what ultimately carry the most weight when my performance is being evaluated. The metrics and expectations tied to my role are what matter most, and that's where my focus needs to be.

So my opportunity is to pause on trying to do everything else, stay in my lane, and execute my role at a high level. If I want to be perceived as a high performer, I need to spend less energy on things outside my scope and more energy on the responsibilities and metrics I'm actually being measured against.
